Continuous Wavelet Transform based CNN Model 
for EV Battery State of Charge Estimation 
Prasanta Kumar Mohanty, Premalata Jena, Narayana Prasad Padhy 
 Department of Electrical Engineering  
IIT Roorkee, Roorkee, India  
pkumarmohanty@ee.iitr.ac.in, premalata.jena@ee.iitr.ac.in, nppadhy@ee.iitr.ac.in 
 
Abstract— The paper introduces a novel approach called 
Continuous Wavelet Transform -based Convolutional Neural 
Network ( CWT-CNN) for precisely estimating electric vehicle 
battery state -of-charge (SOC). SOC holds significant 
importance in analysing vehicle range and ensuring battery cell 
balancing. To validate the effectiveness of the proposed model, 
three real-world datasets are utilised for both the training and 
testing stages.  Incorporating the Continuous Wavelet 
Transform (CWT) in the proposed CWT-CNN model plays a 
vital role. CWT enables extracting essential features from the 
input data by analysing signals in both time and frequency 
domains. This process aids in capturing significant patterns and 
variations that might not be easily discernible through 
conventional methods.  To evaluate the accuracy of the CWT -
CNN model's predictions on the test set data, two evaluation 
metrics, Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute 
Error (MAE), are employed. The reported highest percentage 
values of RMSE (1.26%) and MAE (0.76%) demonstrate the 
effectiveness of the proposed CWT -CNN model in accurately 
estimating the state -of-charge (SOC) of electric vehicle 
batteries.  
Keywords—Electric vehicle, Deep learning, State-of-charge, 
Wavelet transform, Convolutional neural network. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
The necessity of electric vehicle integration in the 
transportation sector arises from its crucial role in mitigating 
air pollution. The energy storage system employed in these 
vehicles is pivotal  in determining their cost, performance, 
size, and safety. In contemporary times, Lithium-ion batteries 
have gained significant preference in electric vehicles owing 
to their superior energy efficiency.  The performance of a 
battery is influenced by a multitude of parameters, including 
battery cell voltage, current, state of charge (SOC), cell 
temperature, ambient temperature, and more. These factors, 
in turn, have a direct impact on various aspects , such as the 
vehicle's range, the cell balancing process, e nergy 
consumption, and the overall battery life.  In battery 
management systems, battery SOC (State of Charge) is a key 
factor. Since the battery's SOC has a significant impact on 
both its general performance and efficiency, determining an 
accurate estimate of the SOC is of the utmost importance. 
 In addition to conventional model -driven approaches, 
data-driven AI techniques are a key feature of contemporary 
literature. These AI -driven techniques are becoming more 
and more well -liked, and this is largely because of their 
extraordinary capacity to handle massive amounts of data 
effectively. These AI-powered, data-driven tools process and 
analyze massive volumes  of information, producing more 
precise and insightful outcomes. Due to their success in 
managing complicated and high-dimensional data, they have 
consequently become a preferred option for numerous 
applications extending across various sectors. 
Furthermore, these methods have proven to be more 
precise in predicting a wide range of outcomes, thus 
becoming invaluable in battery SOC estimation. For instance, 
in [1], the researchers utilized a 1D CNN model for SOC 
estimation. Additionally, in [2], they employed a deep LSTM 
model, achieving impressive RMSE and MAE values of 
0.0239 and 0.0202, respectively. In [3], a Gated Recurrent 
Neural Network (GRU) model was employed, and the 
corresponding RMSE values were found to be 2.15, 1.30, and 
1.34 for 0, 25, and 45 degrees, respectively.  In [4], authors 
explored multiple Artificial Neural Network models, such as 
Support Vector Machines, CNN, and Gated Recurrent Neural 
Networks. They discovered that the CNN model 
outperformed others, yielding RMSE and MAE values of 
1.74 (±0.269) and 1.44 (±0.216), respectively.  Furthermore, 
in [5], a CNN-based model was proposed and optimised using 
specific parameters for improved performance.  However, a 
crucial aspect overlooked in the aforementioned literature  is 
the significance of extracting intricate temporal and 
frequency characteristics from input data, particularly when 
dealing with limited datasets. 
This paper introduces a novel CWT -based CNN model, 
called CWT-CNN, designed for precise electric vehicle 
battery state of charge (SOC) prediction. The Continuous 
Wavelet Transform (CWT) is employed to extract complex 
temporal and frequency characteristics from the input data , 
and its resulting output is subsequently fed into a CNN model 
for the SOC prediction task. The paper highlights three main 
contributions: 
1) The utilisation of CWT enhances prediction 
accuracy, even with limited input data, eliminating 
the need for complex models in SOC prediction.

II. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY 
In this section, three subsections are included, namely the 
"Proposed CWT-CNN Model," "Input Data," and 
"Evaluation Metrics." This section provides a comprehensive 
understanding of the proposed methodology for precise 
battery SOC prediction, utilizing input parameters such as 
measured voltage, measured current, and the ambient 
temperature under which the experiments were conducted. 
A. CWT-CNN Model 
Fig. 1 illustrates the complete architecture of the proposed 
CWT-based CWT-CNN model. Section II (B) provides 
details about the input dataset. The raw dataset is input into 
the continuous wavelet transform (CWT) block, which 
generates a scalogram map representing time -frequency 
representation images. These images are capable of capturing 
low frequencies with improved frequency resolution and high 
frequencies with enhanced time resolution, owing to the 
variable scale wavelets used  [6]. Moreover, the scalogram 
wavelet transform demonstrates superior analytical 
capabilities when dealing with signals exhibiting highly 
distorted shapes.  Fig. 2  displays four randomly selected 
scalogram images from the comprehensive input training 
dataset. These images are generated using the CWT block 
applied to the dynamic test profile data referred to as DST.  
These are 2D images having both spital and temporal 
information of the input time series 1D datasets. 
After generating the scalogram images, they are 
subsequently fed into the CNN regression model for battery 
SOC prediction [7]. The CNN model proposed in this study 
consists of three sets of consecutive four-layer pairs. Each of 
these pairs comprises the following layers: a convolution 
layer, a batch normalization layer, a Rectified Linear Unit 
(ReLU) layer, and a Max pooling layer . This architecture is 
designed to effectively extract and learn important features 
from the input data, contributing to accurate predictions in the 
task of estimating the state -of-charge (SOC) of electric 
vehicle batteries.  These layer combinations are designed to 
extract and learn more intricate features present in the 
scalogram images, enhancing the model's ability to make 
accurate SOC predictions.  Following the extraction of 
features from the previous layers, a dropout layer is 
incorporated, which helps in regularizing the model and

B. Input Dataset 
The input dataset utilized in this paper comprises publicly 
available datasets provided by the CALCE battery research 
team [8]. The Li-ion battery model specifications are detailed 
in Table I. The dynamic test profile data consists of measured 
voltage and measured current profiles recorded at three 
distinct temperatures (0, 25, and 45 degrees Celsius). These 
profiles are obtained using three driving cycles named DST, 
US06, and FUDS. The actual SOC profiles are generated and 
recorded using those voltage, current, and ambient 
temperature profiles. Fig. 3 (a -c) shows the current, voltage, 
and actual SOC profile for the US06 dataset. Similarly, Fig. 
3 (d-f) shows the current, voltage, and actual SOC profile for 
the FUDS dataset. The training data for the model consists of 
the current and voltage profiles from the DST data at 0 
degrees Celsius. On the other hand, the parameters from the 
US06 and FUDS data are used for separate testing of the 
model. It is also to be noted that the highest value of the 
battery SOC is set to 80%.

III. SIMULATION RESULTS 
This section presents the validation of the proposed CWT-
CNN model through simulation results. The validation 
process begins by examining the training outcomes, followed 
by the analysis of the final SOC comparison graphs. This 
comprehensive evaluation ensures the model's effectiveness 
and accuracy in predicting battery SOC.  The training data 
used in this study consists of the measured current and 
measured voltages recorded at an ambient temperature of 0 
degrees Celsius, employing the DST driving cycles. On the 
other hand, the testing dataset comprises SOC values from 
approximately 10,000 samples taken from three dataset s: 
DST, US06, and FUDS, all obtained  at an ambient  
temperature of 25 degrees Celsius. This division enables the 
model to learn from the training data and subsequently  
evaluate its performance on unseen data from different  
driving cycles and temperature conditions.  In Fi g. 4, the 
training Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and loss function 
values for the DST dataset are depicted. It is evident that both 
values consistently decrease and reach levels well below 0.1 
after the 100th iteration. This significant reduction in RMSE 
and loss function values demonstrates the effectiveness of the 
proposed model in accurately predicting the battery SOC for 
the DST dataset. 
After completing the training process, the model is 
subjected to testing using three previously unseen datasets to 
predict the battery SOC values. The SOC comparison results 
are displayed in Fi g. 5. It is evident from Fig. 5 that the 
predicted SOC values closely replicate the actual values for 
all three different datasets. This indicates the robustness and 
accuracy of the proposed model in generalizing to new data 
and making reliable predictions a cross different driving 
cycles and ambient temperatures.  Additionally, to assess the 
model's performance, evaluation metrics such as RMSE and 
MAE percentages are computed and presented in Table II. 
Remarkably, the highest values of RMSE and MAE  
percentages are determined to be 1.26% and 0.76%, 
respectively. These values are considerably lower when 
compared to existing techniques, indicating the superior

IV. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE 
This paper introduces a novel and advanced deep learning 
technique called the CWT -CNN model. Unlike traditional 
CNN models, the CWT-CNN model incorporates a dedicated 
Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) -based feature 
extraction block before the CNN network.  This unique 
addition enables the model to effectively capture essential 
spatial and temporal features from the complex input data, 
enhancing its accuracy and efficiency in predicting the state 
of charge (SOC) of electric vehicle batteries.  Indeed, the 
maximum percentage values of RMSE (1.26%) and MAE 
(0.76%) achieved in the evaluation of the proposed model 
provide strong evidence of its effectiveness. These low 
percentage values indicate that the model's predictions are 
highly accurate and closely aligned with the actual values of 
the battery's state of charge (SOC).  The future prospects of 
this paper encompass two main dimensions. Firstly, there is 
potential for investigating the influence of battery aging, 
degradation, and external factors (such as weather conditions 
and driving behavior) on battery SOC. Integrating these 
factors into the prediction models would enhance the model's 
practicality and accuracy. Secondly, employing transfer 
learning to reduce computational time during long-term SOC 
prediction scenarios could significantly optimize battery 
usage and management. By adopting transfer learning 
techniques, the model can efficiently adapt and improve its 
performance for long-term forecasting tasks, making it more 
practical and applicable in real-world applications.
